# SYS-2 — the home-screen widget

*8 September 2026. What was built for SYS-2 of `docs/reports/2026-09-07-ux-design-plan.md`, and the
four decisions taken while building it that the plan did not settle.*

---

## What it is

A Glance app widget: what you are listening to, a transport under it, and the *Continue listening*
shelf under that. Tapping the body opens the app with the player already up.

It is the first surface in this app that this app does not draw. Glance compiles a composition into
`RemoteViews`, which the **launcher** inflates in its own process — so not one component in
`:core:designsystem` can appear on it. No `WaveScrubber`, no `PlayPauseButton`, no
`PodcastArtwork`: the progress line is the platform's, a button is a tinted `Image` in a rounded
box, and a cover is a `Bitmap` that had to be fetched *before* the tree crossed the process
boundary. The only part of the design system that travels that far is the palette, which is why
`citronLightScheme` and `citronDarkScheme` stopped being `internal`.

Six files carry it:

- `app/widget/WidgetSnapshot.kt` — what the widget shows, as arithmetic. The whole design is here.
- `app/widget/WidgetEntryPoint.kt` — how a widget reaches a Hilt graph, and the flow it draws from.
- `app/widget/NowPlayingWidget.kt` — the drawing.
- `app/widget/WidgetActions.kt` — the five things its buttons do.
- `app/widget/NowPlayingWidgetReceiver.kt` — the name the platform knows it by.
- `res/xml/now_playing_widget_info.xml` — its sizes, and why it asks for no periodic update.

## Four decisions the plan left open

**D-20 — Idle is the normal case, and the widget answers it with `resumableQueue`.** A home screen
is looked at when the app is *not* running: no process, no player, nothing to ask. The widget could
draw nothing then, which makes it blank exactly when it is worth a glance. It could work out what
the user would carry on with, which would be a *third* answer to a question the launcher's *Resume*
shortcut and the system's own playback-resumption tile already answer. It shows that same answer
instead — the head of the durable queue, read through
`PlaybackQueueSource.resumableQueue()` — and the button that starts it calls `EpisodePlayer.resume()`,
which is the same call the shortcut makes. Two surfaces, one answer, and they cannot disagree
because there is only one of them.

**D-21 — Loaded and *next* are different states, and the transport is what tells them apart.**
`WidgetSnapshot.isLoaded` is not `episode != null`, and the reason is what the buttons would mean.
A loaded episode has a position, a play/pause state and a queue around it, so it gets skip back,
play/pause, skip ahead. An episode that is merely next has none of those — skipping forward inside
something that is not playing moves nothing — so it gets one button, and that button says *Resume
listening*. Drawing a full transport for it would be three buttons of which two are decoration.

**D-22 — Progress is whole percent, and that is a cost decision, not a rounding.**
`PlaybackState` re-emits twice a second while playing. Every snapshot that differs from its
predecessor is a `RemoteViews` tree crossing into the launcher's process, so a snapshot carrying the
raw fraction would be a widget redrawing itself a hundred times a minute to move a bar by a pixel it
does not have. At whole percent an hour-long episode redraws every thirty-six seconds, the
`distinctUntilChanged` in `widgetSnapshots()` swallows the rest, and the bar looks the same. Two
tests state both halves of this: half a second of playback must produce an *equal* snapshot, and a
minute of it must not.

**D-23 — The skip glyphs carry no number, and the spoken label carries no number either.** The
app's own skip buttons draw the user's chosen interval inside the arc — 10, 15, 30, 45, 60 — and
PL-7 was written because the mini player's did not, and therefore lied at four of the five settings.
A widget button is forty dp on a home screen, and two digits inside a circle that size are not
legible; ten drawables to say something nobody can read is the wrong trade. So the glyph is a bare
arc, which is true at every setting, and the content description says *"Skip ahead by your skip
interval"* rather than naming a number that would have to be kept in step with a preference the
widget would otherwise have no reason to read. The button itself uses the user's own interval, as
every other skip in the app does.

## Three things that were not obvious

**Reading the live player from a widget is free here, and would not have been in another app.**
The widget collects `PlaybackConnection.playbackState` inside its own composition, which is how it
follows playback without a poll. That normally means binding a `MediaController` — and binding a
`MediaSessionService` starts it. It costs nothing in this app because `NowPlayingPublisher` already
holds a permanent subscription to the same flow for the watch, from `Application.onCreate`. The
widget joins a connection that exists for the life of the process either way. If the watch bridge
is ever removed, this is the second thing that breaks.

**`updatePeriodMillis` is zero on purpose.** The platform's minimum is half an hour, which is both
far too slow to follow an episode and far too often to redraw something that has not changed. The
widget is pushed to, not polled. `NowPlayingWidgetProviderTest` pins the zero, because a value that
looks like an omission is a value somebody will fill in.

**A cover has to be a `Bitmap` before it crosses the boundary.** `Artwork` fetches through the app's
own Coil loader, so a cover the library screen has already downloaded comes off the same disk cache
and over the same `OkHttpClient` the application built. Failure is null and the placeholder stands
in: a widget with a placeholder is a widget, and a widget that threw is a launcher showing "Problem
loading widget". The fetch is capped at 256 px because a `RemoteViews` bitmap crosses an IPC
boundary with a size limit, and five covers at full resolution is how a widget gets dropped.

## Tests

- `WidgetSnapshotTest` (`:app`) — eleven cases against `widgetSnapshot`, which is the whole design
  as arithmetic: playing, paused, closed-with-something-to-resume, freshly installed; a resumable
  episode never reporting itself as playing; the shelf never repeating the episode above it — for
  either reason it could be there — and the shelf being trimmed *after* that removal rather than
  before, which is the difference between four covers and three. The last two are D-22's.
- `NowPlayingWidgetTest` (`:app`) — seven cases rendering `WidgetBody` through
  `runGlanceAppWidgetUnitTest`, which is a real Glance composition on the JVM with no launcher.
  The transport changing shape between loaded and next; play becoming pause; the empty state; each
  shelf cover carrying *its own* episode id on its action rather than a position; and the compact
  rendering dropping the shelf and keeping the buttons. Every button is asserted to reach the
  `ActionCallback` it should, because a callback is wired by class name and nothing else in the
  build checks that the name is the right one.
- `NowPlayingWidgetProviderTest` (`:app`) — four cases against the manifest and
  `now_playing_widget_info.xml`, in the spirit of `LaunchShortcutTest`: the receiver is registered
  for `APPWIDGET_UPDATE`, the declared minimum size is not smaller than the smallest tree the widget
  draws, the update period is zero, and it can be resized in both directions. Nothing in the build
  connects that XML to the code, and a widget declared smaller than it draws is clipped on a home
  screen with no error anywhere.

**What has no test.** The artwork fetch, which needs a network and a launcher to be worth anything —
the composition takes the placeholder branch under test, which is the branch a show without a cover
takes on a device. And the widget's *appearance*: Glance renders `RemoteViews`, not pixels, so the
screenshot suite cannot reach it. Both are for the device pass.

## Dependencies

`androidx.glance:glance-appwidget` and `glance-material3` at **1.2.0**, plus
`glance-appwidget-testing` on the test classpath. `gradle/verification-metadata.xml` grew by 99
lines and nothing was removed. The diff was read: the six Glance artifacts and their two internal
protobuf ones, `androidx.core:core-remoteviews` (Glance's compatibility layer), and metadata-only
entries for `androidx.collection` 1.4.3 and `androidx.datastore` 1.0.0 — older versions Glance
depends on, whose `.module` files Gradle fetches to resolve the conflict even though the newer ones
win. Nothing unexpected.

## Not done here

**The device pass.** §5 asks for one before any of this merges, and this item cannot be judged
without a home screen: whether the compact face is legible at two cells, whether the covers on the
shelf are large enough to recognise a show by, and whether the widget follows a pause pressed in the
notification shade as promptly as it should.

**The stale-face case.** If the process is killed *without* the playback service being stopped —
a low-memory kill, a crash — the launcher keeps the last tree it was given, which may show a pause
button for an episode that is no longer playing. Pressing it binds the service, finds nothing
playing and does nothing; the next snapshot corrects the face. It is rare and self-correcting, and
worth writing down rather than fixing speculatively: the fix would be a broadcast from
`PlaybackService.onDestroy`, and `onDestroy` is exactly what does not run in the case that causes
it.

With this, **P3 has three items left: ADD-3 (OPML), MOM-2 and DS-8** — all three independent, and
each fine to take on the day its screen is open.
